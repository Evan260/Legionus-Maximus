LEGIONUS MAXIMUS: ANSWERS FOR PLAY CONSOLE'S APP CONTENT FORMS

Play Console > Policy and programs > App content (some help pages call it Policy > App content). The forms are below
in the order Play Console lists them, each with the answer to click and why. They were checked against Google's help
pages on 24 November 2026. Play Console words some questions differently from the help pages, and it changes them now
and then, so where a form reads differently, answer by the meaning given here.

What the answers rest on, from the code and the built bundle (builds/android/Legionus Maximus.aab):
- The game itself sends nothing anywhere: no HTTP, sockets, analytics, crash reporting or ads anywhere in its code.
- Saves, settings and whether the full game is owned are files on the phone (user://).
- Google Play Billing sells the one product, full_game. The game asks Play only for the price and whether it is owned.
- The SlowSunday phone plugin carries the Play Games library and notifications for Farmus Maximus, but this game
  never calls either: its Play Games app id is blank, so Play Games never starts (the plugin removes its start-up
  provider from the manifest), and it schedules no notifications.
- Permissions in the bundle's manifest: INTERNET and ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE (from the Play Billing Library),
  com.android.vending.BILLING, POST_NOTIFICATIONS and RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED (from the plugin, unused here), and the
  app's own DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION (from AndroidX). No AD_ID.


1. PRIVACY POLICY

Answer: the address of privacy.html once it is published. Fill it in later.
Why: every app needs one, even one that collects nothing. It must be a public web page, not a PDF, and must name the
developer shown on the listing; the page names Slow Sunday Games.
Also needed: Google wants the policy linked inside the game too, and the game has no such link yet. A "Privacy
policy" button in the settings that opens the page meets Google's rule and Apple's.


2. APP ACCESS (the help pages now call this "Sign-in details")

Answer: All functionality in my app is available without any special access.
Why: there is no login. The full game is unlocked by an ordinary Google Play purchase, which is not an account.


3. ADS

Answer: No, my app does not contain ads.
Why: no ads of any kind, and no ad library in the bundle.


4. CONTENT RATING

Email address: the address IARC should send the rating certificate to.
Category: Game.

Violence: Yes.
  Combat is the whole game. Answer the follow-up questions this way:
  - Against human or human-like characters: Yes. Roman soldiers and barbarian warriors fight and fall, including
    historical leaders such as Arminius and Boudica.
  - Against animals: Yes. Wolves, bears, boars, hounds, crocodiles and war elephants fight for the barbarians and
    fall, and the legion's war dogs fight for Rome.
  - Realistic: No. Small pixel-art figures seen from above, fighting on their own while the player only steers the
    commander. The setting is historical, and the gods' powers (lightning, an earthquake, a rain of fire) are fantasy.
  - The player's side kills: Yes, hundreds of enemies a run, with a kill count on screen.
  - Fire: huts in a barbarian camp burn, the sacred grove is burned, and fire pots, Greek fire and Vulcan's rain of
    fire burn warriors.
  - Gore, dismemberment, decapitation: No. A fallen warrior is knocked back, topples and fades.
  - Torture, cruelty, or violence against the helpless: No.
Blood: Yes.
  When a warrior falls, a few red pixels spray along the blow and a small stain stays on the ground for 5 to 8
  seconds (a boss sprays more, and a chieftain's stain lasts 20). It is on by default, and the Blood setting turns it
  off, showing a puff of dust instead. Answer for blood on. Not gore.
Fear (content that may frighten young children): No.
  No horror and no jump scares. The battles are busy rather than frightening.
Sexuality (nudity, sexual content, suggestive themes): No.
Language (profanity, crude or offensive words): No.
  The tablinum's note on the onager names the wild ass it is called after, the animal.
Controlled substances: Yes, a reference to alcohol only.
  One line of history in the tablinum says Egyptian priests fed a sacred crocodile on cakes, meat and wine. Nobody
  drinks, and there are no drugs or tobacco. The violence sets the rating anyway, so this changes nothing.
Crude humour: No.
Gambling: No, neither simulated gambling nor real money.
  Chests and supply carts give rewards in play, but nothing is bought or staked.
Users can interact or exchange content (chat, voice, sharing pictures): No.
  Single player.
Shares the user's location with other users: No.
Digital purchases: Yes.
  One in-app purchase, full_game, unlocks the whole game. If asked about random items such as loot boxes: No.
Swastikas or other hate symbols, if asked: No.
  Roman eagles and standards only.
Web browser or search engine, if asked: No.

Expected result (IARC and each authority decide): ESRB Teen (Violence, Blood), or Everyone 10+ (Fantasy Violence,
Mild Blood) if ESRB weighs the pixel art; PEGI 12 (Violence); USK 12; IARC 12+; GRAC 12+ or 15+; ClassInd 12 or 14;
ACB PG or M; all with an In-Game Purchases notice.


5. TARGET AUDIENCE AND CONTENT

Target age groups: 13-15, 16-17 and 18 and over.
Why: a combat game with blood, which matches its expected Teen and 12+ ratings. Under 13 is not an option for it:
the Families policy requires everything children can reach in the game to suit children.
If Play asks whether the store listing could unintentionally appeal to children: No.
  A battle game with a screen full of soldiers and barbarians.

What choosing an under-13 group would bring in, under the Families policy (shown for completeness):
- The content would have to suit the youngest group chosen, which the battles and blood do not.
- The game and every library in it must meet COPPA, GDPR and the other children's privacy laws, and use only APIs
  and SDKs approved for child-directed use.
- No advertising ID, device identifiers or location from children (the game already sends none).
- Purchases must make clear that they cost real money; ads only from a Families self-certified ads SDK (there are
  none).
- Google reviews the audience claim, which can take 7 days or longer, and checks every update against these rules.

8. DATA SAFETY

Does your app collect or share any of the required user data types? No.
Why: the game sends nothing off the phone, and what it knows about the purchase stays in a file on the phone, which
is not "collection" in Google's sense. The Play Billing Library talks to Google itself, and Google's Data safety help
says that for a payment service such as Google Play's billing system "you don't need to declare collection of the
data that the payment service collects in connection with its processing of financial transactions", as long as the
app never sees that data and the service collects it under its own terms, which is the case here. The Play Games
library never starts, so it sends nothing. The Steam statistic for barbarians defeated exists only in the Steam
builds.
Play then skips the other questions, and the listing reads "No data collected" and "No data shared with third
parties".


9. GOVERNMENT APPS

Answer: No, my app is not developed by or on behalf of a government.


10. FINANCIAL FEATURES

Answer: My app doesn't provide any financial features.
Why: selling the full game through Google Play is not a financial feature; the list is banking, loans, payments,
crypto and the like.


11. HEALTH

Answer: My app doesn't provide any health features. (Some versions of the form say "My app does not have any
health features".)


12. ADVERTISING ID

Answer: No, my app does not use advertising ID.
Why: the bundle's manifest has no com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID and nothing reads the ID. Play checks the
uploaded bundle's manifest against this answer.


FORMS PLAY ONLY SHOWS WHEN THEY APPLY (none apply)

- Permissions declaration: not needed. POST_NOTIFICATIONS, RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED, INTERNET and BILLING are not on
  Google's list of restricted permissions.
- Exact alarms, foreground services, full-screen intents, photo and video access: none in the bundle.


HOW LEGIONUS DIFFERS FROM THE OTHER THREE

- The only one with combat and blood: Yes to violence and blood, a Teen or 12+ rating instead of Everyone, and no
  question of a child audience.
- Store art must not show graphic violence prominently (Google's metadata policy), so keep blood out of the icon,
  feature graphic and the first screenshots.
- Like Fishus and Mosaicus, no notifications and no Play Games, so Data safety is always "No data collected".
  Farmus changes once its Play Games app id is set.
