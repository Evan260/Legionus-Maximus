# Legionus Maximus

A survivors-style game in pixel art where the player steers a growing Roman legion through barbarian hordes. Fourth game from Slow Sunday Games. The game is in `game/`.

  - `--hard`, `--endless`, `--decimatio` start the run in that mode
  - `--win-at=S` with `--autoplay` wins the run at that second, for a look at the triumph and the unlocks on the summary
  - `--screen=camp|quartermaster|tablinum|map|commanders|settings|title|pause|summary` opens a screen with a sample profile (`--page=controls` for the key page of the settings)
  - `--god=jupiter|neptune|mars|vulcan|apollo` with `--autoplay` calls that god at `--god-at=S` (default 2); `--god=choose` fills the favour and holds the choice of gods open

## Layout

- Autoloads (`project.godot`): `Profile` (`profile.gd`, the saved profile), `Lang` (`lang.gd`, languages and fonts), `Audio` (`audio.gd`, sounds and the two music layers), `Steamworks` (`steam.gd`, achievements and rich presence).
- `game/scripts/sim.gd`: the whole run as plain data (legion, horde, missiles, coins, levels). No nodes, so tests drive it directly. `legion.gd` is the soldiers and their formation, `horde.gd` the barbarians as packed arrays, elites, chieftains, chests and supply carts.
- `game/scripts/terrain.gd`: the props on the field as plain data, seeded per run: scatter, winding forest ridges (`_ridge()`, swept over one tree lattice shared by the whole field, numbers in `forest` in balance.json) and lines of rocks and logs. Trees, rocks and logs are solid; `refresh()` builds a grid of the solid ones around the commander and the horde reads it directly in `_move` for speed. `push_out()` with a time step records chopping on trees, `fell_ready()` turns trees chopped long enough into stumps (`felled`), and `posts` holds the palisade's and the Gaulish king's stakes, in the order they rot. A province with an entry in `fields` in balance.json (Gaul) has its own scatter and, in place of ridges, villages, east and west hedgerows and wheat fields (`_farmland()`); a village or hedge is drawn with no solid of its own and made solid by unseen blockers (kind `""`) over the ground it covers. `clear()` empties the field for tests. It also keeps a route map (`update_flow()`, a breadth-first search over 32 px squares, redone when the commander moves a square) that a barbarian who bumps into a solid prop follows round it. Ravines (`ravine()`, one per 1800 px square, numbers in `ravines`) are gorge centre lines with half widths; `refresh()` lists their segments in a second grid (`rv_*`) that `push_out`, the horde and the route map read, and `bridges` holds the decks laid over them (`Sim.build_bridge`). `--go-to=ravine` or `--go-to=bridge` with `--autoplay` starts at the lip of the nearest ravine, and `--go-to=ravine-end` just past one of its ends, for a look. `--go-to=field` stands the legion in the wheat field nearest the start (with `--province=gaul`).
- The pursuit (`pursuit` in balance.json, `Horde._pursue`): once a second the horde weighs the ground the commander marches for each kill against the stream; a legion that keeps moving while its arms do the killing, or never moves at all, raises `horde.pursuit` towards `cap`. From 1 up, beasts and sprinters (`_hunt`, carrying no coins and left out of the kill rate) come in ahead of its march and from all round it, tougher and fiercer as it climbs, the bosses come sooner and more of them may be alive. A legion that holds and fights (the facing bots) keeps it at 0.
- Gaul opens by winning in Germania (`Content.province_before()` passes over provinces whose art is missing). Its enemies' abilities are keys in content.json read by `horde.gd`: the swordsman's `slash`, horseman's `flank_charge` (he rears on impact), archer's `volley` (a ring in `foe_lobs`), boar's `rush` (stunned by anything solid), champion's `duel`, horn blower's `rally` heal, `blast` and `flee`, and the king's `sworn` guard, `siege` stakes and `summon`, with his guards released in his second phase.
- `game/scripts/objectives.gd`: the camp, stranded column and sacred grove placed in clearings at the start, and the fort that appears with the chieftain at `chieftain_at`; their huts, oaks and walls are terrain props (`Terrain.add_clearing`). `--objectives-near` places them close and `--go-to=camp|column|grove|fort` starts beside one (inside the fort), and `--go-to=burning-camp` in the camp's ring so it burns at once, for a look.
- `game/scripts/arms.gd`: the twelve weapons and six forged forms, on their timers, with burning ground, oil, caltrops and the stake ring as zones.
- `game/scripts/upgrades.gd`: the level-up choices, rerolls and banishes.
- `game/scripts/gods.gd`: the favour of the gods, which fills with kills, the choice of three of the five gods and what each one does, as plain data the sim updates; their numbers are under `gods` in balance.json.
- `game/scripts/content.gd`: reads `game/data/content.json` (every recruit, arm, forged form, drill, enemy, province, commander, quartermaster upgrade and achievement, with its name, text, history and art keys). Grounds are `game/assets/grounds/<province>.png`, found by `available_art()` as `ground:<province>`, so a province turns on with no code change once its ground, props and enemies exist. An entry whose art keys are not in the atlas or the icons folder stays hidden, so content can be built before its art.
- `game/data/balance.json`: every number. `credits.json` feeds the credits.
- `game/scripts/world_view.gd`, `hud.gd`, `atlas.gd`: drawing. Every sprite is in one atlas so the horde draws in one batch. The ground and Gaul's wheat fields are drawn on two layers behind the world view, the wheat through `wheat_field.gdshader`, which frays each field's edge with noise (drawing only; the fields' rectangles in `terrain.gd` are unchanged).
- `game/scripts/main.gd`: input, the state machine and the command-line modes. `game.gd` says whether this is the demo.
- `game/scripts/screens/`: title, camp, quartermaster, tablinum, map, commanders, pause, settings and the run summary, all drawn through `ui.gd`. `controls.gd` has the default keys and pad buttons and the player's rebinding laid over them.
- `game/tests/run_tests.gd`: every test. `WAITING_FOR_ART` (recruits) and `NO_ICON_YET` (arms, forged forms, drills, empty since every icon exists) list entries whose art is not drawn yet; shrink them as art lands.
- `game/scripts/lang.gd` (autoload `Lang`): the 14 languages, their fonts and the saved or system language. Every player-facing string goes through `tr("English words")`, and `content.json` names, texts and histories get a key such as `content.recruits.legionary.name` that `Content.localize()` lays over the English. `--lang=CODE` shows another language.
- Translations: `python tools/extract_strings.py` fills `game/translations/strings.csv` (and clears the cells of a line whose English changed), `python tools/translate_strings.py --yes` sends the empty cells to Azure (key in `.env`) and then writes the hand-checked cells of `tools/translation_fixes.json` over the machine's (its glossary keeps names such as the gods' in each language's form; put every hand fix there, never only in the CSV), `python tools/write_presence.py` writes `tools/steam/rich_presence.vdf`; reimport the project afterwards. English, French, German, Spanish, Italian and Portuguese draw in Silkscreen, Polish, Turkish, Russian and Ukrainian in Fusion Pixel 10 px (16 px at large text), Chinese, Japanese and Korean in Fusion Pixel 8 px. Draw text through `Ui.font()` and `Ui.px`, and wrap it with `Ui.lines_of`, which breaks between letters in Chinese and Japanese. Key hints are built in `Controls.hint()` from a key and one translated word each (`Controls.HINT_WORDS`), so no translated string holds a key.

## Art

All art is PixelLab, made with `tools/art/pixellab.py` (token in `.env`, never in code), costs in `tools/art/ledger.csv`.

- Characters are 48 px (the berserker, war band leader and war priest 80 px, the chieftain 112 px, all from their `_big` folders), low top-down, selective outline, from 8 directions. Walking and running use PixelLab's built-in `walking` and `running` motions, which loop and cost nothing.
- Ground and props are made with Pro Flash using `tools/art/raw/style_ref.png` (the commander and a barbarian) as the style image, or they come out in a brighter cartoon style that does not match.
- `tools/art/batch_art.sh` makes the prototype's art, `build_atlas.py` packs it into `game/assets/atlas.png` (4096 px wide, refused past 4096 tall) and takes new units and props through its `UNITS` and `PROPS`, a unit's art folder through `FOLDERS`, and an "attack" action wherever `attack_<direction>` folders hold frame_00 to frame_08; `make_seamless.py` and `remove_leaves.py` make the ground tile and `make_boot_splash.py` the boot splash.
- No mirroring: every direction is drawn.
- Look at every still before paying to animate it, and try one direction of a new animation before paying for all eight.
- Animals and riders use `--template dog`, `bear` or `horse`. The `horse` and `bear` templates save each picture under the direction one step clockwise of the one drawn, so rename the stills to what they show and keep PixelLab's own label in `character.json` as `pixellab_direction`.
- Icons use pixen with "inventory item icon of a single object: ... Object only, no people, no faces, no creatures", or it draws a figure. Ask for no symbols or crosses on props.
- When jobs run in parallel, log each job's own cost in `ledger.csv`, not the whole balance drop.

## Rules

- The horde is packed arrays with a flat grid; keep per-enemy work out of dictionaries, and check `--bench` after touching `_move_enemies`.
- Packed arrays copy when changed through another container, so change them by name.

## Release

- Pushing a tag like `v0.1.0` runs `.github/workflows/release.yml` ("Release builds"). The tag must match `config/version` in `game/project.godot` or the run stops. It can also be started by hand with `gh workflow run "Release builds"`.
- Jobs: the tests, then a full and a demo export for Windows, macOS and Linux, then macOS signing, then a GitHub release for tags. Demo builds come from the "... demo" presets, which carry the `demo` feature (`OS.has_feature("demo")`).
- Mac builds ship unsigned until the Apple secrets (`MACOS_CERTIFICATE_P12` and the rest, see `.github/scripts/sign_macos.sh`) are set on the repo.
- Steam uploads stay off until `tools/steam/steam.json` has real app and depot ids and the repo variable `STEAM_UPLOAD` is `true`. Every id is 0 for now.
- Local export: `python tools/export.py [--demo] [windows|macos|linux]`.
