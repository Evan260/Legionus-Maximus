# Legionus Maximus

A survivors-style game in pixel art where the player steers a growing Roman legion through barbarian hordes. Fourth game from Slow Sunday Games. The game is in `game/`.

## Layout

- Autoloads (`project.godot`): `FullGame` (`full_game.gd`, first, the one Google Play purchase that opens the full game on a phone, copied unchanged from Farmus Maximus as the SlowSunday Android Plugin's README says; on a computer it has no store, so nothing is for sale or locked), `Profile` (`profile.gd`, the save slots and the settings), `Lang` (`lang.gd`, languages and fonts), `Audio` (`audio.gd`, sounds, and the music: the battle tracks `music/battle_*.ogg` played one after another from the moment the game opens, through the menus and runs alike, with any music in the player's own folder `user://music/`, which the settings open), `Steamworks` (`steam.gd`, achievements and rich presence).
- Saves (`profile.gd`): three save slots, `user://save_1.json` to `save_3.json`, each a game of its own: denarii, quartermaster ranks, unlocks, records, the codex, totals, achievements and the camp's choices (`data.choices`: province, commander, difficulty). The player's settings (volumes, full screen, shake, damage numbers, blood, text size, language, keys and buttons) are shared by every slot in `user://settings.json` (`Profile.settings`, read through `Profile.setting()`, written by `save_settings()` as soon as a setting changes, through `settings.json.tmp`, with the last readable settings kept as `settings.json.bak`, and either is read when `settings.json` is missing or damaged). Steam achievements stay with the Steam account. So does the Steam stat `kills` (an INT set by the client, the progress stat of `kills_1000`, `kills_10000` and `kills_100000` in Steamworks): it is read at start-up once Steam answers `requestUserStats`, each finished run adds its kills to it before the save banks them (`Steamworks.bank_kills`), and the kill achievements count the larger of it and the slot's `totals.kills` (`Steamworks.lifetime_kills()`), so a new slot or another computer starts from the account's count and a game without Steam from the slot's; Steam's progress pop-up shows each time a run reaches another tenth of a target (`steam.progress_steps`). Each save is written to a `.tmp` file and read back before it replaces the save, the last saves are kept as rolling backups `.bak1` to `.bak3` (moved along at most every 5 minutes), the newest readable of the save, its `.tmp` and its backups is loaded, and a slot none of whose files can be read is set aside as `.unreadable-STAMP` copies rather than saved over. A save whose `version` is above `Profile.VERSION` was made by a newer build (the demo and the full game share the folder): the load list shows it, saved by a newer version, but it is never opened or saved over, and every save is laid over the file as it was read (`Profile.laid_over`), so fields this build does not know are kept. When a save fails, the camp and the summary say "Your progress could not be saved" (`Profile.last_save_failed`) until one succeeds. The game opens the slot saved last. The title offers Continue (the open game), New game (the first free slot; with all three taken the player deletes one under Load first, and a game is never written over) and Load, the list of slots with Play and Delete (`screens/saves.gd`), which asks before deleting and deletes only once the word "delete" is typed (the translated word or the English one), as in the other Slow Sunday games; while the question is open every key goes into the word (`SavesScreen.capture`), Escape cancels and Enter deletes. The single `user://profile.json` of earlier builds is moved on the first start into slot 1 and `settings.json`, and kept whole as `profile.json.migrated`; this runs only while every slot is free. Tests use `user://test_saves` (`_fresh_profile()`).
- `game/scripts/sim.gd`: the whole run as plain data (legion, horde, missiles, coins, levels). No nodes, so tests drive it directly. `legion.gd` is the soldiers and their formation, `horde.gd` the barbarians as packed arrays, elites, chieftains, chests and supply carts.
- `game/scripts/terrain.gd`: the props on the field as plain data, seeded per run: scatter, winding forest ridges (`_ridge()`, swept over one tree lattice shared by the whole field, numbers in `forest` in balance.json) and lines of rocks and logs. Trees, rocks and logs are solid; `refresh()` builds a grid of the solid ones around the commander and the horde reads it directly in `_move` for speed. `push_out()` with a time step records chopping on trees, `fell_ready()` turns trees chopped long enough into stumps (`felled`), and `posts` holds the palisade's, the wall's and the Gaulish king's stakes, in the order they rot. Wall stakes (`build_wall()`, kind `"wall"`, marked `Terrain.WALL` in `solid_tree`) stand behind the rear rank, and barbarians walking into one batter it (`batter()`, `hold` seconds each, bosses `boss_batter` times faster) instead of slipping through, so a wall across a narrow gap holds the horde back for a while; a crowd ringing a wall breaks in after about 20 to 25 seconds. A province with an entry in `fields` in balance.json (Gaul) has its own scatter and, in place of ridges, villages, east and west hedgerows and wheat fields (`_farmland()`); a village or hedge is drawn with no solid of its own and made solid by unseen blockers (kind `""`) over the ground it covers. `tools/art/build_atlas.py` brings the hedge pieces' greens to the oak's (`MATCH_GREEN`). Wheat fields can be walked through, by the legion and the horde alike, while their province's `solid` is off as it is in Gaul; with it on, each field's rectangle, set `field_inset` px in from its drawn edge with rounded corners (`field_solids()`), is listed in a third grid (`fd_*`) that `push_out`, the horde, Neptune's wave and the route map read, `out_of_fields()` keeps spawns out of the wheat, and a legion marching head-on into a field turns along its edge (`round_field()`). A field is cut back (`_fit_field()`) to keep a lane of `field_lane` px from the next field and from every hedge and house, so Gaul is never a maze. `clear()` empties the field for tests. It also keeps a route map (`update_flow()`, a breadth-first search over 32 px squares, redone when the commander moves a square) that a barbarian who bumps into a solid prop follows round it. Ravines (`ravine()`, one per 1800 px square, numbers in `ravines`) are gorge centre lines with half widths; `refresh()` lists their segments in a second grid (`rv_*`) that `push_out`, the horde and the route map read, and `bridges` holds the decks laid over them (`Sim.build_bridge`). The men file into rows for a deck (`Legion._column`) only while the commander is on it, heading for it within `bridge_aim` and `bridge_approach` px past the formation's front, or just stepping off one he crossed. The fort's gate uses the same rule from outside, so fighting along its wall keeps the formation. The HUD's edge badges (`Hud.targets_of`) point to the objectives, the fort and the living chieftain; `--boss=ID --boss-act=far` brings a chieftain in stunned off screen for a look at his badge. `--go-to=ravine` or `--go-to=bridge` with `--autoplay` starts at the lip of the nearest ravine, and `--go-to=ravine-end` just past one of its ends, for a look. `--go-to=field` stands the legion beside the wheat field nearest the start (with `--province=gaul` or `aegyptus`). A province with an entry in `mountains` (Dacia) swaps the scatter, the ridge tree (pines) and the line kinds (rock only) and raises the ridge and line chances; one with an entry in `streams` gets frozen streams laid out like ravines (`stream()`, kept clear of them), on which the legion slides (`Sim.glide`, `ice` in balance.json) and so do the barbarians (`Horde.e_slide`, the `foe_*`, `boss_grip` and `heavy_grip` numbers), all but the chieftain; `refresh()` sorts the ice near the commander into squares (`ice_cell`, `ice_bank`) that `on_ice()` and the horde read. `--go-to=stream` starts above the nearest one. A province with an entry in `deserts` (Parthia) has no ridges or lines: a region holds an oasis of palms, a ruined wall line (solid, sometimes breached), a spread of dunes or open sand (`_desert_feature()`), and dunes are soft sand that slows the march (`Terrain.on_dune`, `Sim.on_sand`). A province listed in `tree_drift` (Parthia, whose ground is sand) draws a small drift of sand under the foot of every tree and stump, in its ground tile's own colours and fitted to where the trunk meets the ground in the atlas, with a faint shadow to the east (`WorldView._paint_drifts()`); trees on other ground have none. `--go-to=palms` there starts beside the nearest palm. A province with an entry in `moors` (Britannia) has heather scatter and few ridges (`ridge_chance`); its other regions hold a stone circle, a spread of bog pools or a patch of heather (`_moor_feature()`). A circle is a ring of `stone_circle` pictures, each made solid by unseen blockers on its seven stones, with `circle_gap` of them left out as its opening; trees of ridges nearby are kept out of it (`_henges`). Bogs slow the march more than dunes (`Terrain.in_bog`, `Sim.in_bog`) and block nothing. `--go-to=circle` or `--go-to=bog` starts beside the nearest one. A province with an entry in `hills` (Hispania) has dry grass, rock and olive scatter and a few ridges of olives (`tree`, `ridge_chance`); its other regions hold an olive grove planted in rows, one or two dry-stone terrace walls or a patch of dry grass (`_hill_feature()`). Olives are trees, solid and felled like oaks; a terrace wall is a line of solid `stone_wall` pieces running roughly east and west (`wall_tilt`), and every wall has one or two gaps of `wall_gap` px kept away from its ends, so it is never a maze. Dry grass is drawn on the ground and blocks nothing. `--go-to=terrace` or `--go-to=olives` starts beside the nearest wall or olive. A province with an entry in `canals` (Aegyptus) has irrigation canals in place of ravines: straight parallel lines that run across the whole field, all one way per run (east and west or north and south, tilted by up to `tilt`), `apart` px from one another, with the start between two of them at least `start_clear` from either (`set_canals()`, `_canal_line()`). Each canal region lays the part of every canal that crosses it (`_canal_region()`), each step of a line belonging to the region its middle lies in, so the pieces meet end to end. Bridge decks stand every `bridge_every` px along each canal (`_bridge_along()`, listed once in `bridges` by canal and number, whichever region is built first), nothing solid stands within `bridge_clear` of a deck's ends (`_by_bridge()`), and clearings never remove a canal: an objective or the fort still by one moves across to the middle of its strip of land (`strip_middle()`). Canals block and drown like gorges, so the legion files over the bridges by the bridge rules and can still bridge a canal with timber. The water (`assets/water.png`, drawn by `python tools/art/make_water.py` with no PixelLab) is laid on its own layer behind the world view, where `water.gdshader` drifts it `drift` px a second along the canals, and a band of wet mud lines both banks. One with an entry in `valleys` (Aegyptus) has stone and date palm scatter and no ridges; a region holds a grove of date palms (trees, solid and felled), a bed of papyrus reeds on the nearest canal bank, a ruined temple (an obelisk and fallen columns, each column made solid by unseen blockers, every stone `ruin_gap` px from the others) or open ground (`_valley_feature()`). Its grain grows in wheat fields drawn like Gaul's and walked through like them, laid by the canals rather than by region: each bank of each stretch of canal between two bridges holds one field with chance `grain_chance` (`_grain_field()`), `grain_length` long and `grain_depth` deep, starting `grain_bank` px past the bank's band and keeping `grain_bridge` px from each bridge, so the bank is broken at every crossing. A field's rectangle is kept in a frame turned through `Terrain.field_turn` (the canals' angle, 0 in Gaul; `field_box()` gives the world rectangle round it), and `wheat_field.gdshader` turns it back, so the field lies along a tilted canal; `grain_tint` tints the wheat a little greener than Gaul's. Groves, temples and scatter are kept out of the fields (`_in_grain()`). Reeds are drawn standing and seen through, block nothing and slow the march a little (`Terrain.in_reeds`, `Sim.in_reeds`, `reed_slow`). `--go-to=canal|reeds|ruin|palms` starts beside the nearest crossing, reed bed, obelisk or date palm.
- The pursuit (`pursuit` in balance.json, `Horde._pursue`): once a second the horde weighs the ground the commander marches for each kill against the stream; a legion that keeps moving while its arms do the killing, or never moves at all, raises `horde.pursuit` towards `cap`. From 1 up, beasts and sprinters (`_hunt`, carrying no coins and left out of the kill rate) come in ahead of its march and from all round it, tougher and fiercer as it climbs, the bosses come sooner and more of them may be alive. A legion that holds and fights (the facing bots) keeps it at 0.

## Art

All art is PixelLab, made with `tools/art/pixellab.py` (token in `.env`, never in code), costs in `tools/art/ledger.csv`.

- Characters are 48 px (the berserker, war band leader and war priest 80 px, the chieftain 112 px, all from their `_big` folders), low top-down, selective outline, from 8 directions. Walking and running use PixelLab's built-in `walking` and `running` motions, which loop and cost nothing.
- Ground and props are made with Pro Flash using `tools/art/raw/style_ref.png` (the commander and a barbarian) as the style image, or they come out in a brighter cartoon style that does not match.
- `tools/art/batch_art.sh` makes the prototype's art, `build_atlas.py` packs it into `game/assets/atlas.png` (8192 px wide, kept to one page so the horde draws in one batch, refused past 8192 tall) and takes new units and props through its `UNITS` and `PROPS`, a unit's art folder through `FOLDERS`, and an "attack" action wherever `attack_<direction>` folders hold frame_00 to frame_08; `make_seamless.py` and `remove_leaves.py` make the ground tile and `make_boot_splash.py` the boot splash.
- No mirroring: every direction is drawn.
- Look at every still before paying to animate it, and try one direction of a new animation before paying for all eight.
- Animals and riders use `--template dog`, `bear` or `horse`. The `horse` and `bear` templates can save a picture under another direction than the one it shows, often but not always one step clockwise, so check every still by eye, rename it to what it shows and keep PixelLab's own label in `character.json` as `pixellab_direction`.
- Icons use pixen with "inventory item icon of a single object: ... Object only, no people, no faces, no creatures", or it draws a figure. Ask for no symbols or crosses on props.
- When jobs run in parallel, log each job's own cost in `ledger.csv`, not the whole balance drop.

## Rules

- The horde is packed arrays with a flat grid; keep per-enemy work out of dictionaries, and check `--bench` after touching `Horde._move`.
- Packed arrays copy when changed through another container, so change them by name.

## Release

- Pushing a tag like `v0.1.0` runs `.github/workflows/release.yml` ("Release builds"). The tag must match `config/version` in `game/project.godot` or the run stops. It can also be started by hand with `gh workflow run "Release builds"`.
- Jobs: the tests (which also fail on any SCRIPT ERROR line, and when `tools/extract_strings.py` would change the sheet), then a full and a demo export for Windows, macOS and Linux, then macOS signing, then a GitHub release for tags. Demo builds come from the "... demo" presets, which carry the `demo` feature (`OS.has_feature("demo")`).
- The demo (the `demo` feature, `--as-demo`, or a phone that has not bought the full game) plays only `demo.province` in balance.json (Germania), lists the first `demo.commanders` (3) commanders and ends each run at `demo.seconds` (660, 11 minutes), through `game.gd`.
- The demo build holds only what the demo shows, so changing its `demo` feature tag gives nothing more. `tools/export.py --demo` (and so `publish.py demo` and the workflow's demo jobs, which install Pillow, numpy and OpenCV for it) exports it from a copy of `game/` that `tools/demo_stage.py` makes in a temporary folder. The copy keeps `demo.province` with its enemies and the ones they bring, the first `demo.commanders` commanders, every recruit, arm, forged form, drill, god and quartermaster upgrade, and the achievements naming only those; content.json, balance.json and the translations lose the rest. `build_atlas.py --cut` cuts the copy's atlas from `game/assets/atlas.png` as it stands, so new art needs no step of its own. The tablinum still shows the other provinces' enemies as dark shapes (`Content.glimpses`), so each keeps its south-facing outline, filled with one colour. A prop is left out when only other provinces name it: in their `props` or `art` in content.json, their blocks in balance.json, the fort pieces on their ground, or a name starting with such a prop's (`hedge_end_left`); terrain.gd's `SCATTER` stays. The other grounds are left out, and the ice, the canal water and the wheat tile, which the code loads in every province, become one clear pixel. `data/demo_build.json` (`Game.DEMO_MARK`) keeps the copy the demo whatever its feature tags say. Before the export, `game/tools/demo_props.gd` lays out the province's field and fort on the copy for a few seeds and stops it when a prop placed there, one of the province's enemies or a listed commander is not drawn. Every demo export is then checked by `tools/check_demo.py`, which fails when the build holds another province's ground, enemies, props or data, or an outline with more than one colour; it also takes any built demo (`python tools/check_demo.py BUILD`). `python tools/demo_stage.py FOLDER` writes the copy to look at. The copy leaves out `game/android/`, and the Android exports never use it: a phone ships the whole game and the purchase opens it in place.
- Mac builds ship unsigned until the Apple secrets (`MACOS_CERTIFICATE_P12` and the rest, see `.github/scripts/sign_macos.sh`) are set on the repo.
- Steam uploads stay off until `tools/steam/steam.json` has real app and depot ids and the repo variable `STEAM_UPLOAD` is `true`. Every id is 0 for now.
- Local export: `python tools/export.py [--demo] [--out DIR] [windows|macos|linux]` (into `builds/` unless `--out` names another folder).
