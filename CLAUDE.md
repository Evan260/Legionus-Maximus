# Legionus Maximus

A survivors-style game in pixel art where the player steers a growing Roman legion through barbarian hordes. Fourth game from Slow Sunday Games. The game is in `game/`.

## Layout

- Autoloads (`project.godot`): `Profile` (`profile.gd`, the save slots and the settings), `Lang` (`lang.gd`, languages and fonts), `Audio` (`audio.gd`, sounds, and the music: the battle tracks `music/battle_*.ogg` played one after another from the moment the game opens, through the menus and runs alike, with any music in the player's own folder `user://music/`, which the settings open; the harp loop `calm.ogg` is only in the trailer), `Steamworks` (`steam.gd`, achievements and rich presence).
- Saves (`profile.gd`): three save slots, `user://save_1.json` to `save_3.json`, each a game of its own: denarii, quartermaster ranks, unlocks, records, the codex, totals, achievements and the camp's choices (`data.choices`: province, commander, difficulty). The player's settings (volumes, full screen, shake, damage numbers, text size, language, keys and buttons) are shared by every slot in `user://settings.json` (`Profile.settings`, read through `Profile.setting()`, written by `save_settings()` when the settings close). Steam achievements stay with the Steam account. Each save is written to a `.tmp` file and read back before it replaces the save, the last saves are kept as rolling backups `.bak1` to `.bak3` (moved along at most every 5 minutes), the newest readable of the save, its `.tmp` and its backups is loaded, and a slot none of whose files can be read is set aside as `.unreadable-STAMP` copies rather than saved over. The game opens the slot saved last. The title offers Continue (the open game), New game (the first free slot; with all three taken the player deletes one under Load first, and a game is never written over) and Load, the list of slots with Play and Delete (`screens/saves.gd`), which asks before deleting. The single `user://profile.json` of earlier builds is moved on the first start into slot 1 and `settings.json`, and kept whole as `profile.json.migrated`; this runs only while every slot is free. Tests use `user://test_saves` (`_fresh_profile()`).
- `game/scripts/sim.gd`: the whole run as plain data (legion, horde, missiles, coins, levels). No nodes, so tests drive it directly. `legion.gd` is the soldiers and their formation, `horde.gd` the barbarians as packed arrays, elites, chieftains, chests and supply carts.
- `game/scripts/terrain.gd`: the props on the field as plain data, seeded per run: scatter, winding forest ridges (`_ridge()`, swept over one tree lattice shared by the whole field, numbers in `forest` in balance.json) and lines of rocks and logs. Trees, rocks and logs are solid; `refresh()` builds a grid of the solid ones around the commander and the horde reads it directly in `_move` for speed. `push_out()` with a time step records chopping on trees, `fell_ready()` turns trees chopped long enough into stumps (`felled`), and `posts` holds the palisade's, the wall's and the Gaulish king's stakes, in the order they rot. Wall stakes (`build_wall()`, kind `"wall"`, marked `Terrain.WALL` in `solid_tree`) stand behind the rear rank, and barbarians walking into one batter it (`batter()`, `hold` seconds each, bosses `boss_batter` times faster) instead of slipping through, so a wall across a narrow gap holds the horde back for a while; a crowd ringing a wall breaks in after about 20 to 25 seconds. A province with an entry in `fields` in balance.json (Gaul) has its own scatter and, in place of ridges, villages, east and west hedgerows and wheat fields (`_farmland()`); a village or hedge is drawn with no solid of its own and made solid by unseen blockers (kind `""`) over the ground it covers. `tools/art/build_atlas.py` brings the hedge pieces' greens to the oak's (`MATCH_GREEN`). Wheat fields can be walked through, by the legion and the horde alike, while their province's `solid` is off as it is in Gaul; with it on, each field's rectangle, set `field_inset` px in from its drawn edge with rounded corners (`field_solids()`), is listed in a third grid (`fd_*`) that `push_out`, the horde, Neptune's wave and the route map read, `out_of_fields()` keeps spawns out of the wheat, and a legion marching head-on into a field turns along its edge (`round_field()`). A field is cut back (`_fit_field()`) to keep a lane of `field_lane` px from the next field and from every hedge and house, so Gaul is never a maze. `clear()` empties the field for tests. It also keeps a route map (`update_flow()`, a breadth-first search over 32 px squares, redone when the commander moves a square) that a barbarian who bumps into a solid prop follows round it. Ravines (`ravine()`, one per 1800 px square, numbers in `ravines`) are gorge centre lines with half widths; `refresh()` lists their segments in a second grid (`rv_*`) that `push_out`, the horde and the route map read, and `bridges` holds the decks laid over them (`Sim.build_bridge`). The men file into rows for a deck (`Legion._column`) only while the commander is on it, heading for it within `bridge_aim` and `bridge_approach` px past the formation's front, or just stepping off one he crossed. The fort's gate uses the same rule from outside, so fighting along its wall keeps the formation. The HUD's edge badges (`Hud.targets_of`) point to the objectives, the fort and the living chieftain; `--boss=ID --boss-act=far` brings a chieftain in stunned off screen for a look at his badge. `--go-to=ravine` or `--go-to=bridge` with `--autoplay` starts at the lip of the nearest ravine, and `--go-to=ravine-end` just past one of its ends, for a look. `--go-to=field` stands the legion beside the wheat field nearest the start (with `--province=gaul`). A province with an entry in `mountains` (Dacia) swaps the scatter, the ridge tree (pines) and the line kinds (rock only) and raises the ridge and line chances; one with an entry in `streams` gets frozen streams laid out like ravines (`stream()`, kept clear of them), on which the legion slides (`Sim.glide`, `ice` in balance.json). `--go-to=stream` starts above the nearest one. A province with an entry in `deserts` (Parthia) has no ridges or lines: a region holds an oasis of palms, a ruined wall line (solid, sometimes breached), a spread of dunes or open sand (`_desert_feature()`), and dunes are soft sand that slows the march (`Terrain.on_dune`, `Sim.on_sand`). A province with an entry in `moors` (Britannia) has heather scatter and few ridges (`ridge_chance`); its other regions hold a stone circle, a spread of bog pools or a patch of heather (`_moor_feature()`). A circle is a ring of `stone_circle` pictures, each made solid by unseen blockers on its seven stones, with `circle_gap` of them left out as its opening; trees of ridges nearby are kept out of it (`_henges`). Bogs slow the march more than dunes (`Terrain.in_bog`, `Sim.in_bog`) and block nothing. `--go-to=circle` or `--go-to=bog` starts beside the nearest one. A province with an entry in `hills` (Hispania) has dry grass, rock and olive scatter and a few ridges of olives (`tree`, `ridge_chance`); its other regions hold an olive grove planted in rows, one or two dry-stone terrace walls or a patch of dry grass (`_hill_feature()`). Olives are trees, solid and felled like oaks; a terrace wall is a line of solid `stone_wall` pieces running roughly east and west (`wall_tilt`), and every wall has one or two gaps of `wall_gap` px kept away from its ends, so it is never a maze. Dry grass is drawn on the ground and blocks nothing. `--go-to=terrace` or `--go-to=olives` starts beside the nearest wall or olive. A province with an entry in `canals` (Aegyptus) has irrigation canals in place of ravines: straight parallel lines that run across the whole field, all one way per run (east and west or north and south, tilted by up to `tilt`), `apart` px from one another, with the start between two of them at least `start_clear` from either (`set_canals()`, `_canal_line()`). Each canal region lays the part of every canal that crosses it (`_canal_region()`), each step of a line belonging to the region its middle lies in, so the pieces meet end to end. Bridge decks stand every `bridge_every` px along each canal (`_bridge_along()`, listed once in `bridges` by canal and number, whichever region is built first), nothing solid stands within `bridge_clear` of a deck's ends (`_by_bridge()`), and clearings never remove a canal: an objective or the fort still by one moves across to the middle of its strip of land (`strip_middle()`). Canals block and drown like gorges, so the legion files over the bridges by the bridge rules and can still bridge a canal with timber. The water (`assets/water.png`, drawn by `python tools/art/make_water.py` with no PixelLab) is laid on its own layer behind the world view, where `water.gdshader` drifts it `drift` px a second along the canals, and a band of wet mud lines both banks. One with an entry in `valleys` (Aegyptus) has crop, stone and date palm scatter and no ridges; a region holds a grove of date palms (trees, solid and felled), a bed of papyrus reeds on the nearest canal bank, a ruined temple (an obelisk and fallen columns, each column made solid by unseen blockers, every stone `ruin_gap` px from the others) or a field of crops in rows along the nearest canal bank within `crop_bank`, on one side or both, slid along the bank clear of the bridges (`_crop_field()`); crops grow nowhere else (`_valley_feature()`). Reeds are drawn standing and seen through, block nothing and slow the march a little (`Terrain.in_reeds`, `Sim.in_reeds`, `reed_slow`). `--go-to=canal|reeds|ruin|palms` starts beside the nearest crossing, reed bed, obelisk or date palm.
- The pursuit (`pursuit` in balance.json, `Horde._pursue`): once a second the horde weighs the ground the commander marches for each kill against the stream; a legion that keeps moving while its arms do the killing, or never moves at all, raises `horde.pursuit` towards `cap`. From 1 up, beasts and sprinters (`_hunt`, carrying no coins and left out of the kill rate) come in ahead of its march and from all round it, tougher and fiercer as it climbs, the bosses come sooner and more of them may be alive. A legion that holds and fights (the facing bots) keeps it at 0.
- The road runs Germania, Gaul, Britannia, Hispania, Aegyptus, Parthia, Dacia (the order of `provinces` in content.json), and its legs on the map never cross (`MapScreen.ROUTES`, checked by a test).
- A province opens by winning the drawn one before it on the road (`Content.province_before()`, `Profile.open_won_provinces()`); the map shows it locked with what opens it until then, and a click on it shows that in the panel with the back sound. Each run has a difficulty, `run_setup.difficulty` (`Sim.DIFFICULTIES`: easy, medium, hard), saved with the game like the province and chosen in the map's panel: Medium is the game as tuned, and Easy and Hard scale every enemy's health (`health_mult`), the stream of ordinary enemies (`spawn_mult`), the denarii (`denarii_mult`) and, where the block sets `damage_mult` (Easy and Medium do), every enemy's damage, by the `easy` and `hard` blocks in balance.json (`Horde.level_mult()`), on top of the province's own `difficulty`. A win on any difficulty counts as won and opens the next province; a win on Hard also sets `hard_won`, the red pennant on the map. The `crowd` block in balance.json caps the ordinary enemies on the screen or within `margin` of it at 70, 100 and 200 by difficulty (`Horde.crowd`, counted each step in `_spawn`): the stream slows from half that number and stops at it, and surges, war bands, hunters and summons are cut to fit (`Horde.full()`), while elites and the chieftain still come. Every enemy walks at `enemy_speed_mult` (0.9) of the speed content.json lists. An enemy on foot left beyond `give_up_radius` (600) and out of view gives up the chase and is removed (chariots and riders, `Horde.k_rides`, keep circling), with no kill or coin, so marching away is a real escape; `solid_range` is set past it so every enemy still chasing bumps into trunks and no crowd comes out of a tree line without felling it. At most `bosses.max_alive` (3) timed bosses are alive at once, and the pursuit neither hastens nor adds bosses (`pursuit.boss_haste` and `pursuit.bosses` are 0). Any soldier picks up a chest. The horn (`audio.kinds.surge`) has a 45 second cooldown, since hunts and the grove send bands every few seconds. Medium (20 November 2026) sends 80% of the enemies with 85% of their health and damage and pays 1.25 times the denarii; Easy and Hard set `xp_mult` so that per coin they level as fast as Medium. The first supply cart of a run shows a notice saying what carts give, and each opened cart names its reward. Endless and Decimatio open by winning in the province.

The Egyptian spearman's spear is a `thrust` and his tall shield an `armour` against arrows, the Nubian archer's arrows a `volley` from long range, the khopesh raider `sprint`s in and `slash`es, and the crocodile is a slow `beast` with `share` 0.5 whose `rush` is a short lunge started only within `reach` of the legion. The priest of Sobek's one ability is `harden` (`Horde.harden`: the warriors round him take `cut` less harm until `e_ward`, drawn green), and the camel captain's is a `wedge` of Parthian camel riders through `_onrush`, so walls, trees and the testudo stop it. The Egyptian general (Achillas) has a `cleave`, `volleys` (`Horde.volleys`: a notice, then `rings` marked over the legion, one on the commander, where `volley` foe lobs fall after `delay` seconds and hurt soldiers and commander alike, each man once however many of the rings he stands in; more rings in his second phase) and `sluice` (`open_sluices`: a notice and a band `width` px wide marked across the legion's path `ahead` of it, which floods after `warn` seconds for `seconds`, slowing the march while the commander stands in it (`Horde.flood_slow`) and hastening the warriors near the legion). Below half health he fights faster and releases his guard of spearmen.
- `game/scripts/objectives.gd`: the camp, stranded column and sacred grove placed in clearings at the start, and the fort that appears with the chieftain at `chieftain_at`, which can be held only once he has fallen; holding it then wins the run, his death alone never does; the triumph after a win lasts until every barbarian and coin is gone and every man is in his place, between `triumph.seconds` and `triumph.most_seconds`, and a win moves the map to the next province (`Game.next_province`); the fort is a Roman camp of palisade pieces, corner watchtowers and rows of tents round the yard (`_fort_pictures`), its walls held by blockers with no picture; the wall pictures are two seamless strips, `wall_tile.png` laid edge to edge from tower to tower and in behind the gate, and `side_tile.png` stacked up each side, both made from the palisade picture by `tools/art/make_castra_walls.py`; their huts, oaks and walls are terrain props (`Terrain.add_clearing`). `--objectives-near` places them close and `--go-to=camp|column|grove|fort` starts beside one (inside the fort; `--go-to=fort-gate` south of it, marching in through the gate), and `--go-to=burning-camp` in the camp's ring so it burns at once, for a look. The grove's ring of trees or stones is scenery with no solid part, since a legion standing at the altar is wider than the ring, and the first time the commander comes within `grove.tip_within` a notice says what burning it gives.
- `game/scripts/arms.gd`: the twelve weapons and six forged forms, on their timers, with burning ground, oil, caltrops and the stake ring as zones.
- `game/scripts/upgrades.gd`: the level-up choices, rerolls and banishes.
- `game/scripts/gods.gd`: the favour of the gods, which fills with kills, the choice of three of the five gods and what each one does, as plain data the sim updates; their numbers are under `gods` in balance.json.
- `game/scripts/content.gd`: reads `game/data/content.json` (every recruit, arm, forged form, drill, enemy, province, commander, quartermaster upgrade and achievement, with its name, text, history and art keys). Grounds are `game/assets/grounds/<province>.png`, found by `available_art()` as `ground:<province>`, so a province turns on with no code change once its ground, props and enemies exist. An entry whose art keys are not in the atlas or the icons folder stays hidden, so content can be built before its art.
- `game/data/balance.json`: every number. `credits.json` feeds the credits.
- `game/scripts/world_view.gd`, `hud.gd`, `atlas.gd`: drawing. Every sprite is in one atlas so the horde draws in one batch. The ground and Gaul's wheat fields are drawn on two layers behind the world view, the wheat through `wheat_field.gdshader`, which frays each field's edge with noise (drawing only; the solid ground is each field's rectangle set in from its edge in `terrain.gd`).
- `game/scripts/main.gd`: input, the state machine and the command-line modes. `game.gd` says whether this is the demo.
- `game/scripts/screens/`: title, the load list, camp, quartermaster, tablinum, map, commanders, pause, settings and the run summary, all drawn through `ui.gd`. `controls.gd` has the default keys and pad buttons and the player's rebinding laid over them.
- `tools/steam/`: the store page's pictures (`make_store_art.py`, rendered by the game through `store_scene.gd`), the achievement icons (`make_achievement_icons.py`, into `store/achievements/`), the rich presence file and `publish.py`, which uploads builds once `steam.json` has real ids. The store text is in `store/steam_page/`.
- `tools/trailer/`: `make_trailer.py` stages each shot through `game/tools/trailer/` (`--trailer-shot`) and cuts them together with `edit.tscn`; `make_scroll.py` draws the end card's scroll. `docs/Trailer-plan.md` is the plan.
- `game/tests/run_tests.gd`: every test. `WAITING_FOR_ART` (recruits) and `NO_ICON_YET` (arms, forged forms, drills, empty since every icon exists) list entries whose art is not drawn yet; shrink them as art lands.
- `game/scripts/lang.gd` (autoload `Lang`): the 14 languages, their fonts and the saved or system language. Every player-facing string goes through `tr("English words")`, and `content.json` names, texts and histories get a key such as `content.recruits.legionary.name` that `Content.localize()` lays over the English. `--lang=CODE` shows another language.

Key hints are built in `Controls.hint()` from a key and one translated word each (`Controls.HINT_WORDS`), so no translated string holds a key.

## Art

All art is PixelLab, made with `tools/art/pixellab.py` (token in `.env`, never in code), costs in `tools/art/ledger.csv`.

- Characters are 48 px (the berserker, war band leader and war priest 80 px, the chieftain 112 px, all from their `_big` folders), low top-down, selective outline, from 8 directions. Walking and running use PixelLab's built-in `walking` and `running` motions, which loop and cost nothing.
- Ground and props are made with Pro Flash using `tools/art/raw/style_ref.png` (the commander and a barbarian) as the style image, or they come out in a brighter cartoon style that does not match.
- `tools/art/batch_art.sh` makes the prototype's art, `build_atlas.py` packs it into `game/assets/atlas.png` (8192 px wide, kept to one page so the horde draws in one batch, refused past 8192 tall) and takes new units and props through its `UNITS` and `PROPS`, a unit's art folder through `FOLDERS`, and an "attack" action wherever `attack_<direction>` folders hold frame_00 to frame_08; `make_seamless.py` and `remove_leaves.py` make the ground tile and `make_boot_splash.py` the boot splash.
- No mirroring: every direction is drawn.
- Look at every still before paying to animate it, and try one direction of a new animation before paying for all eight.
- Animals and riders use `--template dog`, `bear` or `horse`. The `horse` and `bear` templates can save a picture under another direction than the one it shows, often but not always one step clockwise, so check every still by eye, rename it to what it shows and keep PixelLab's own label in `character.json` as `pixellab_direction`.
- Icons use pixen with "inventory item icon of a single object: ... Object only, no people, no faces, no creatures", or it draws a figure. Ask for no symbols or crosses on props.
- When jobs run in parallel, log each job's own cost in `ledger.csv`, not the whole balance drop.

## Rules

- The horde is packed arrays with a flat grid; keep per-enemy work out of dictionaries, and check `--bench` after touching `_move_enemies`.
- Packed arrays copy when changed through another container, so change them by name.

## Release

- Pushing a tag like `v0.1.0` runs `.github/workflows/release.yml` ("Release builds"). The tag must match `config/version` in `game/project.godot` or the run stops. It can also be started by hand with `gh workflow run "Release builds"`.
- Jobs: the tests, then a full and a demo export for Windows, macOS and Linux, then macOS signing, then a GitHub release for tags. Demo builds come from the "... demo" presets, which carry the `demo` feature (`OS.has_feature("demo")`).
- The demo (the `demo` feature, or `--as-demo`) plays only `demo.province` in balance.json (Germania), lists the first `demo.commanders` (3) commanders and ends each run at `demo.seconds` (660, 11 minutes), through `game.gd`.
- Mac builds ship unsigned until the Apple secrets (`MACOS_CERTIFICATE_P12` and the rest, see `.github/scripts/sign_macos.sh`) are set on the repo.
- Steam uploads stay off until `tools/steam/steam.json` has real app and depot ids and the repo variable `STEAM_UPLOAD` is `true`. Every id is 0 for now.
- Local export: `python tools/export.py [--demo] [windows|macos|linux]`.
